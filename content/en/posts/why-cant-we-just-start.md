Abraham Lincoln said it: "**Give me six hours to chop down a tree and I will spend the first four sharpening the axe**", and... it seems I took it too personally.

My father calls it the vulture syndrome, because you spend all your time planning and planning without doing anything.

And that is exactly a problem I have been dealing with for a long time, in many areas where I have wanted to express myself or create some kind of content.

Not least with this blog: I have been thinking about creating it since 2025, and it is only in September 2026 that it is getting a life! Never mind other plans I had, like opening a covers channel, another one with music to work to, another one to share content about productivity systems (what a paradox), creating open source projects (at least the zettelkasten skill for your AI agent is already available!).

I was feeling very frustrated about the situation I was in (still a little, to be honest), and this is what I found:

## The myth of laziness: procrastination is emotional regulation

Contrary to popular belief, procrastinating is not a time management problem but a problem of **emotional regulation**. According to the research of Dr. Fuschia Sirois and several studies from the National University of Rosario (UNR), postponing is a mechanism of **experiential avoidance**. It is a desperate attempt by your brain to dodge the discomfort, the anxiety or the sense of incapacity that a specific task generates in you.

"Anxiety is a complex system of behavioral, physiological, affective and cognitive responses... that activates when anticipating events judged as highly aversive because they are perceived as unpredictable and uncontrollable."

To your brain, that pending report is an unpredictable threat. This is where the **3v3 conflict model** proposed by Dr. K (HealthyGamerGG) comes in: your mind steps into an arena where three "obligatory" tasks (studying, cleaning, updating your CV) compete against three immediate gratifications (social media, video games, adult content). The result is a paralysis by analysis where the working memory system saturates and you end up doing nothing.

It is vital to understand that these patterns tend to consolidate during adolescence. The UNR Integrative Project underlines that early academic procrastination does not only affect grades, it impacts **social autonomy** and the individual's interaction in the long term, becoming a burden for adult life.

## The trap of "productive procrastination"

This is where I fall the most: a couple of times a month, or a week? or a day? Let's get to the point:

There is an especially insidious variant: **procrestividad**, or structured procrastination. In that state you keep busy doing "useful" things (organizing files, washing dishes) to avoid the "main course". Experts like Dr. Sharon Saline explain that these finite tasks give us a **false dopamine hit**. They have a clear beginning and a clear end, which makes us feel effective while we avoid the vulnerability of the complex project.

Even the act of "tuning your tools" is a trap. Dr. Maelisa McCaffrey points out how professionals often fall into the loop of "perfecting the template". Changing the format over and over is a way of avoiding the real task; it is a search for safety in the face of the fear of not being good enough.

To tell a personal anecdote: I have spent almost the last 6 months changing my AI harness, my Obsidian vault, even alternating between using Obsidian or Craft Docs, my operating system, my skillset, my PARA system, my Zettelkasten, my music plugins, my DAWs, and even the physical arrangement of my setup, more than 4 times each! and this blog and two AI skills are everything I have actually published since then!

## The fantasy of perfection: the enemy of "good enough"

Perfectionism is not a search for excellence, it is a **fantasy of protection** against failure. As Voltaire said: "Perfect is the enemy of good". The fear that the result will not be impeccable leads us to collect infinite information, which drains our willpower.

## Working memory hacks: the doorway effect and the "next best thing"

To "trick" a blocked brain, Dr. K suggests two neuroscientific techniques:

1. **The doorway effect:** Physically walking to another room. Crossing a threshold helps reset working memory, breaking the loop of internal conflict (the 3v3) you are stuck in in front of the computer.
2. **The "Next Best Thing" (not the smallest one):** Many people say "just start for a minute". But if the water is 100 meters deep, your brain knows that digging one meter is a waste of energy. The cost-benefit is very low. Instead of doing the "minimum", look for the **most valuable action you are capable of taking** in that moment of resistance. Maximize the benefit perceived by your dopaminergic system.

## Toward a productivity with compassion

The key to breaking the paralysis is not military discipline but curiosity and self-compassion. We should accept, as Dr. McCaffrey suggests, that the schedule is fluid and that rest is not "wasting time" but an essential part of the mental health needed to execute.

Procrastinating is not a sign that you are incapable but that you are human and feeling a threat. Instead of judging ourselves, we can ask ourselves:

**What emotion am I trying to avoid today by keeping myself busy with the irrelevant? How would my life change if I gave myself permission to deliver something imperfect but finished, today?**
